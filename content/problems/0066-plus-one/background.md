Adding one to a written number starts at the rightmost digit because that is the least significant place. If the digit there is below 9, the addition ends immediately. If it is 9, it becomes 0 and a carry moves one place left. This is the same carrying rule used when adding any two numbers by hand.

### Everyday examples

- **An odometer.** The last wheel turns from 9 to 0 and pushes the next wheel forward; a row of 9s rolls over to a new leading digit.
- **A countdown timer.** The seconds column wraps from 59 to 00 while the minutes column increments, and the same idea applies to decimal digits.

### How it shows up here

The list is already in the order a person writes a number, so the right end is the ones place. Walk from right to left. The first digit that is not 9 can absorb the carry and stop the walk. If every digit is 9, they all become 0 and a new leading 1 is needed.
