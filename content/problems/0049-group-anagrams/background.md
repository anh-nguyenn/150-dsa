An equivalence class collects items that are considered the same under some rule. Here the rule is "can be rearranged into one another." To group by an equivalence rule, it helps to compute a canonical form: a standard representative that is identical for every item in the class.

### Everyday examples

- **Scrabble tiles.** Racks holding the same multiset of tiles belong to one family, no matter how the tiles are ordered on the rack.
- **Library sorting by title signature.** If every book title were rewritten with its letters in alphabetical order, all rearrangements of the same title would land in the same drawer.

### How it shows up here

The canonical form is the word with its letters sorted. Two words are anagrams exactly when their sorted forms match. A dictionary maps each canonical form to the list of original words that produced it, and the dictionary's values are the answer groups.
